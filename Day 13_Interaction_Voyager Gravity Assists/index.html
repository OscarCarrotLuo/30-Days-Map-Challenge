Projection & methods

Map plane
The J2000 ecliptic viewed from its north pole. The Sun is at (0, 0) in the flight plan. Close pass subtracts the moving planet’s position at every sample, placing its center at the origin. Both axes use the same linear scale. A path leaving the plane appears foreshortened. Point symbols have a minimum size for legibility.
Flight plan
The solid line follows JPL’s reconstructed Voyager 2 ephemeris from 20 August 1977 to 1 January 1992. Before 29 August 1989, Horizons uses a mission-design trajectory that approximately reproduces the encounters. Each cross locates a flyby at its own date. Short ticks along the flight plan mark 1 January.
Orbits
Orbital distances and body radii use astronomical units. The eight planets are plotted at the selected encounter epoch. Their orbit lines are instantaneous two-body ellipses. Fainter lines show a selection of minor-planet orbits at 25 August 1989. The close-pass view shows selected moons and their orbits at closest approach. All curves use the same ecliptic projection.
Sun-only coast
The dashed line starts with Voyager’s 3-D position and velocity 30 days before the selected encounter and follows solar gravity alone for 760 days. The comparison omits planetary forces and maneuvers. It illustrates how the outgoing route changes.
Turn angle
The angle between the incoming and outgoing 3-D velocity vectors relative to the planet, sampled seven days before and after closest approach. The dates and angles follow the reconstructed ephemeris on the TDB time scale.
